Scheduling and assignment 8

State the constraints and a solver returns the table. A feasible answer is guaranteed before optimality is chased.

  • Nurse rostering

    Solves a real roster under rules like an 11-hour rest gap. Before chasing an optimum it makes sure at least one feasible schedule exists under any setting, then tunes fairness and preference as weights on top.

    Constraint optimizationCP-SATLabor rules

  • Contact center staffing

    Derives the agents needed per interval from arrival volume and builds the shifts. Raise the service target one notch and it recomputes how many more people that costs.

    QueueingShift optimization

  • League fixture scheduling

    Builds a league calendar while holding home and away balance and consecutive-game limits. Relax one constraint and it shows how many fixtures get easier.

    Combinatorial optimizationRound robin

  • Preference-aware room assignment

    Assigns people to rooms with both preferred and avoided companions in the model. It puts a capacity-only assignment next to an optimized one and counts the difference.

    Local searchTheoretical bound

  • Airline crew pairing

    Chains flight legs into duty pairings while keeping rule violations at zero. Modeled as set partitioning, and it separates the sizes it can prove optimal from the ones it cannot.

    Set partitioningCP-SAT

  • School timetabling

    Builds a timetable under teacher, room and class constraints at once. Scale it up and it shows exactly where optimality proof stops, rather than hiding it.

    Constraint optimizationScheduling

  • Live dispatch on a real map

    Reassigns drivers while orders keep arriving. It compares dispatch planned once against dispatch rewritten every moment, on an actual map.

    Real-time re-optimizationRouting

  • Test axis coverage

    Counts which combinations your tests actually exercise and finds the blind spots. Pick a preset and it tells you how many defects that configuration cannot catch.

    Combinatorial coverageFault injection

  • Gichul Seoan, an offline desktop app for the Korean History Exam

  • Gamrisa Seoan, an offline desktop app for the Korean IS Auditor exam

    Eleven public rounds of the Korean Information Systems Auditor exam — 1,084 questions — parsed in full and inverted into 1,267 knowledge nodes. Inverting them showed what the exam actually does: it barely reuses knowledge. 1,201 of the 1,267 nodes appear in exactly one question, and the mean round-to-round recurrence is 5.8%. So the denominator for progress moved off the past papers and onto the official 686-topic syllabus, and the app separates the 259 topics the papers touch from the 427 they never do. The bundle carries original exam text under a no-redistribution licence, so the build and repo stay private — only the screens and design record are shown.

    TauriFully offlineSM-2 spaced repetitionThe bundle carries copyrighted exam text, so the build is not distributed

    AggregationMetric definition

  • Demand forecast and ordering

    Calculates how much inventory and how many stockouts a miss actually costs. Raise safety stock and it shows where the cost moves to.

    Time seriesInventory policy

  • Sales signal radar

    ScoringSignal fusion

  • The gate before buying recommendations

    Measures whether a recommender beats a baseline you could build in a week. Across five categories the trained models failed to beat a co-occurrence baseline in three of them, and that result was published rather than dropped.

    Baseline designRecall@K

  • Geo-aware semantic IDs

  • MFT Lens, NTFS forensics that runs in the browser

    Drop a dumped NTFS $MFT CSV and it unfolds all eight $STANDARD_INFORMATION and $FILE_NAME timestamps, flags the records where the two disagree, and stands them up as a MACB timeline. A tampered creation time is only visible where those two sets diverge. Parsing happens entirely in the tab with zero network requests, so the material under investigation never leaves the machine. That property is the product, so the repository stays closed and only the screen is shown.

    NTFS $MFTSI/FN mismatchMACB timelineThe source stays private, so only the screens are shown

The frame runs six tiers over 500 pulls, with expected count, chance of at least one, and expected pulls to get one on every row. The legendary sits at 0.40% and 250 expected pulls.

    Normalised probabilityExpected valueProbability-based tiersThe source stays private, so only the screens are shown

Vision and signal judgment 7

Receivers and inspectors built from antenna to verdict. Judgments in real time, evidence attached.

  • Surface defect inspection

    Shows what changes when a judgment that varies by inspector is pinned to one standard. Move the threshold and it counts missed defects and false rejects together.

    Computer visionThreshold tuning

  • RF interference diagnosis

    Separates interference sources by their signature in the radio environment. Lower the signal to noise ratio and it shows where the judgment collapses.

    Signal processingFingerprinting

  • Medical imaging closed loop

    Traces a reading-assist tool through human confirmation and back into training. Research use, and the screen says so rather than implying diagnostic intent.

    Closed loopHuman review

  • Counting vehicles with a spare phone

    Counts passing vehicles from sound alone, with no camera. Measured on public road recordings, a modern encoder failed to beat a 2015-era energy detector at counting, and that result is published as it came out.

    Acoustic countingSite calibration

  • Air-gapped link over sound

    Moves data between two disconnected devices using near-ultrasound. Turn up reverberation and you can watch exactly where the link breaks.

    Near-ultrasoundM-FSK

  • Air-gapped data diode

    Computes one-way transfer with no return channel, in the browser. The fountain code and peeling decoder were ported to JavaScript, so you can watch 25 percent loss get reconstructed on the spot.

    LT fountain codeOne-way transfer

  • Five comms measurements, from the physical layer to jammer taxonomy

    Modulation recognition, an LDPC decode kernel, channel estimation throughput, transmitter fingerprinting, jammer taxonomy. Each one is operable here: raise the SNR and the verdict moves, grow the batch and the throughput stops climbing, change the seed and the conclusion holds. Every number is a lookup into a table produced by really running it, and the browser only looks things up. Results that failed to reproduce, and figures taken on synthetic benches, say so. The implementations stay private.

    Measured lookup tablesGPU kernel vs public referencesLightGBM
