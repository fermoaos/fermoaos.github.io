Privacy

This site collects nothing about its visitors. No accounts, no logins, no analytics, no ad tracking. Here is exactly what that means.

What we store

Nothing. The site is static files with no server and no database of ours behind it. There is no code here that records your visit on our side.

What stays in your browser

One key, fermoa-theme. It holds whether you chose graph paper or blackboard, and it stays in your browser. It is never sent to us, and clearing it means the next visit follows your system setting.

We use no cookies.

The contact form

The three-step form hands what you typed to your own mail program and opens a draft. You are the one who presses send, and the message travels through your mail provider rather than any server of ours. What we receive is that one email.

We use what is in it to answer that enquiry. We do not move it elsewhere or use it for advertising.

Requests that go to third parties

Opening a page makes your browser reach three places directly: Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com) for the display and body faces, and jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) for the Korean face. Those requests carry your IP address and browser details and are governed by each company's own policy.

The site itself is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub also sees access logs.

The demo links under Use Cases go elsewhere only when you click them. Leave them alone and no request is made.

What is not here

No analytics, tag manager, tracking pixel, ad network, A/B testing tool, session replay or chat widget. None of it. You can check this claim: open your browser's network tab and reload the site — nothing leaves except the three destinations named above.

The replay demo

The agent demo on the homepage replays a recording that ships with the site. It takes no input and makes no outbound request. Pressing the approve button advances the playback and goes nowhere else.

Questions about this policy

Send them to the same address. If the policy changes, the date on this page changes with it.
